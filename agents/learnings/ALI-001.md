# ALI-001: Overnight autonomous run — specs 04-09 of the doc-driven workflow
Date: 2026-07-27
Phase: implementation
Status: draft

**What happened:** Six focused specs were designed, implemented, and tier-2 certified autonomously in one overnight run per the user's standing authorization. All merged green, but four classes of divergence required mid-run correction: contaminated RED baselines, a certification whose evidence chain needed forensic reconstruction, an ambient contract that failed to stop boundary-crossing, and a subagent harness that could not traverse human-approval gates until the dispatch form changed.

## L1: Evaluation channels leak into the system under test
- **What we assumed:** Pointing RED baseline agents at worktree tool paths, and writing dispatch prompts freely, would not contaminate the no-skill baseline.
- **What is actually true:** Two distinct contamination modes appeared: baseline agents read co-located scenario and spec files from the worktree (milestone-to-features RED 01A read its own scenario; RED 03 read spec 05 and cited its ">10 hard limit"), and a dispatch author scripted the expected behavior step-by-step, making 5/6 "complied" verdicts meaningless (execute-milestone first RED batch, discarded).
- **Evidence:** `test-workflow/results/milestone-to-features.md` RED 01A/03 contamination caveats; `test-workflow/results/execute-milestone.md` header disclosure of the discarded first batch (commit `36ee95b`).
- **Why the assumption was wrong:** The isolation preamble constrained where agents could WRITE, but nothing constrained what they could READ or what the dispatch could REVEAL; the evaluation channel was designed for write-isolation only.
- **Class of error:** Evaluation-channel leakage into the system under test
- **Improvement items:**
  - **P1 — Project and product documents:** `docs/specs/workflow/01-testing-and-conformance.md` — erratum codifying the neutral-path RED protocol (tools copied to a scratch dir; dispatches carry the scenario Prompt verbatim and nothing prescriptive) that specs 06-09 converged on ad hoc.
  - **P2 — Engineering and operations:** `test-workflow/TESTING.md` — one paragraph naming the two contamination modes as rerun-invalidating conditions.

## L2: Unarchived dispatch texts turn certification audits into forensics
- **What we assumed:** The results log's recorded observables are sufficient evidence for a certification claim.
- **What is actually true:** When the review-milestone GREEN runs were challenged over step-enumerated dispatch prompts, the verdict could not be settled from the log — the dispatch texts existed only as prose description, and validity was salvaged by fixture forensics (cross-run structural variance proving outcomes were agent-authored, e.g. rm-04a folding two commits where rm-04b split them).
- **Evidence:** `test-workflow/results/review-milestone.md` enumerated-dispatch caveat and the `TESTING.md` review-milestone row note; the adjudication rested on surviving scratchpad fixtures, which are session-ephemeral.
- **Why the assumption was wrong:** Observables prove what happened in the target; they cannot prove what the agent was TOLD, and attribution disputes are exactly about the telling.
- **Class of error:** Unarchived test-harness inputs
- **Improvement items:**
  - **P1 — Verification:** `test-workflow/` — certification runs archive their exact dispatch texts alongside the results log (e.g. `test-workflow/results/dispatches/<scenario>-<run>.md`), so attribution never depends on scratchpad survival.
  - **P2 — Project and product documents:** `docs/specs/workflow/01-testing-and-conformance.md` — add dispatch archival to the results-log contract.

## L3: Descriptive boundary prose does not bind; decision procedures do
- **What we assumed:** Listing human boundaries in the ambient WORKFLOW.md ("Humans ignite: ... feature decomposition ...") suffices to stop a skill-less agent from self-starting planning skills.
- **What is actually true:** Under "keep things moving overnight" pressure, a sonnet agent read the contract, decomposed a milestone anyway (inventing the illegal state `features-ready`), and quoted the boundary ex post facto ("say the word to proceed" — after crossing it). The same class appeared inside execute-milestone: its descriptive guard failed self-ignition until REFACTOR 3 replaced it with a literal-token decision procedure, and the phase-6 probe complied only after the prohibition became an explicit bullet.
- **Evidence:** `test-workflow/results/workflow-e2e.md` phase-6 RED entry with the verbatim ex-post-facto quote; contract fix commit `a470ca7`; execute-milestone REFACTOR history (`cc40e1d`).
- **Why the assumption was wrong:** Descriptive prose asks the agent to infer a prohibition from a role statement; under goal pressure the inference loses. A decision procedure (literal token present? if no: stop) leaves nothing to infer — this repo had already proven that pattern per-skill and still shipped the ambient contract as description.
- **Class of error:** Descriptive rule where a decision procedure is required
- **Improvement items:**
  - **P1 — AI agent assets:** `WORKFLOW.md` — the riding gloss: scope the no-self-start bullet against model-invocable helper skills (write-adr, act-learn-improve) and define "ROADMAP planning states" (19 words of budget headroom).
  - **P2 — Engineering and operations:** skill-authoring guidance (spec 01 or the writing-skills notes) — boundaries ship as decision procedures keyed to observable predicates, never as role descriptions.

## L4: Single-turn workers cannot traverse human-approval gates without standing answers
- **What we assumed:** Scripted human replies listed in a dispatch prompt would be consumed when the agent reached a preview/approval gate.
- **What is actually true:** Subagents END their turn at a wait-for-human gate — there is no second turn — so e2e phase dispatches stalled at session_tx previews (and review-milestone bare invocations earlier produced zero-write completions). The fix was framing: replies conditioned as standing answers already given ("when you reach a preview, the human's standing answer is: approved, commit ... do not end your turn waiting"), which then carried five different skills through their gates unaided in both certified pipelines.
- **Evidence:** `test-workflow/results/workflow-e2e.md` CORRECTION block (orchestrator-executed first attempt voided) and the certified runs' methodology lines (commit `b0fd1f1`); `test-workflow/results/review-milestone.md` bare-invocation caveat.
- **Why the assumption was wrong:** The skills correctly stop at human gates — that is their discipline working; the harness misunderstanding was treating a terminating wait as an interaction point. The gate model and the worker model (single turn) were never reconciled in the scenario-runner design.
- **Class of error:** Interactive-gate vs single-turn-worker mismatch
- **Improvement items:**
  - **P1 — Verification:** `docs/specs/workflow/01-testing-and-conformance.md` — document the standing-answer dispatch form as the canonical scenario-runner protocol for gated skills.
  - **P2 — Verification:** investigate the residual bare-invocation zero-write completions (harness artifact vs skill-triggering weakness) before any tier-3 attempt.

## L5: Seam defects are invisible to per-task review and surface only at whole-branch or pipeline level
- **What we assumed:** Focused specs plus per-task reviews would keep cross-skill seams consistent.
- **What is actually true:** Three seam defects survived every per-task gate and were caught only by whole-branch final reviews or the e2e lane: spec 07's Decision 6 contradicted its own Decision 11 (plan-commit timing), the remediation loop dead-ended because execute-milestone's literal text lagged its spec, and the ambient prohibition list omitted the planning skills entirely.
- **Evidence:** errata in `docs/specs/workflow/07-execute-milestone.md` (two) and the spec-08/09 final-review records; the remediating fix landed as spec 09 Task 1 (`382927f`).
- **Why the assumption was wrong:** Per-task reviewers see one diff against one brief; seams live in the composition of certified artifacts that no single task touches. The whole-branch reviews caught these by design — but only because their dispatch prompts explicitly demanded sibling-consistency checks, which was an ad-hoc controller habit, not a codified step.
- **Class of error:** Cross-artifact seam drift below the review horizon
- **Improvement items:**
  - **P2 — Engineering and operations:** `docs/specs/workflow/01-testing-and-conformance.md` or TESTING.md — codify the sibling-consistency sweep (state vocabulary, transition ownership, tool paths, stop boundaries across all six skills) as a required final-review input rather than a controller habit.
